# Loadouts

The loadout picker the character creator opens from a job's Loadout button, in place of the stock window with one tab
per group. A rail on the left lists every group of the role under a few categories (clothing, head and face, bags,
weapons, tools, extras, rank) with what is picked there and a chip for anything that needs attention. The right side
shows the open group as a grid of tiles, or matches from every group while the search box holds two or more
characters. The header shows what the kit costs against the balance the server will pay from, and links to the first
group that still needs a pick or holds something the character can't afford.

Entry points: `WolfgateLoadoutWindow` (same constructor, events and `RefreshLoadouts` as the stock `LoadoutWindow`, so
the editor only swaps the type), `LoadoutCatalog` (the view-model, built once per window; selecting only re-reads state)
and `LoadoutCategorizer` (which rail section a group goes under).

Groups carry no category, so `LoadoutCategorizer` derives one from the slot most of a group's loadouts equip. Groups
whose items sit in storage or in hand (weapons, tools) are placed by name key in `NameCategories`; add a key there when
a new group lands in Extras that belongs elsewhere. `LoadoutCatalogTest` fails when a key no longer names any group.

The window is stricter than the stock one in two places: a required pick can only be replaced, not dropped, and a full
multi-pick group refuses another pick instead of silently dropping its oldest.

Items that share a name within a group (every headset is "headset") get the part of their entity id the others lack
put in front of the name. That is a stopgap; the real fix is distinct entity names in Fluent.

The window's style rules are in `Content.Client/_WF/Stylesheets/StyleWolfgate.Loadouts.cs`. The stock window under
`Content.Client/Lobby/UI/Loadouts` is left in place and unused, so an upstream change to how it lists or validates
loadouts has to be carried over to `LoadoutCatalog` by hand.
